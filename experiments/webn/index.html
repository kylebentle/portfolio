A Personal Investigation • WEBN Cincinnati

Is WEBN Still Playing The Same #$&% I Was Listening To In High School?

I grew up outside Cincinnati and spent free time driving around in my 1990 Toyota Corolla and listening to WEBN — classic rock, alt-metal, the hits of the early aughts.

I moved away after graduation and didn't lisen much for more than a decade. When I returned in 2019 and flipped on the radio for the first time, something immediately struck me.

"This is all the same stuff that was playing when I was in high school."

Was I right, or was nostalgia playing tricks? I used Claude Code to build a scraper and find out.

01 — The Investigation

Three Weeks of WEBN, Measured

I scraped WEBN's recently-played feed every few minutes for three weeks in February–March 2026, then matched each song against the iTunes and MusicBrainz APIs to get its original release year.

02 — The Verdict

Mostly true. The 90s and 2000s own the airwaves.

More than 60% of every song played on WEBN in these three weeks was released before 2010. The recent spikes — 2025 and 2022 — are hard to miss. But that's just a handful of new songs getting hammered into heavy rotation. My high school years (2001–2005) are highlighted in green.

03 — The Catalog

But the 2000s have the deepest bench

It's not just plays — the station's actual song catalog skews toward my high school years and earlier. Songs from 2005 and earlier account for the overwhelming majority of WEBN's deep catalog. The new stuff — 2020s — is only 41 unique tracks, but they get hammered into rotation (over 15 plays per song on average).

Most played artists have been around for a while

05 — The One Caveat

New songs dominate the top of rotation

The catalog skews old, but the most-played individual songs are almost entirely from the last few years. Radio hammers current releases; the classics get spread thin across hundreds of songs.
